Mixed-carton CBM: prepare dimensions and gross weight for a freight quote

Work through a seven-carton example, record outer dimensions and gross weight, and keep measured packing data separate from chargeable weight and quoted fees.

By Fugia Express · Published 2026-11-20

Updated 2026-11-27 — packing records and revision checks expanded.

Group cartons before calculating volume

Use a separate row for each carton type with the same packing arrangement and outer dimensions. Record the goods description, carton-number range, number of cartons, length, width, height, dimension unit and either individual gross weights or a measured group total. Measurements should describe the package that will actually be handed over. Mark unpacked estimates as estimates and retain the date of the later measurement. A bare product dimension is not a shipping-carton dimension.

A seven-carton example: 0.519 cubic metres

This is a fictional teaching example, not a Fugia shipment or quotation. Group A has four cartons measuring 60 × 40 × 40 cm each. Group B has three cartons measuring 50 × 30 × 30 cm each. For centimetre inputs, calculate length × width × height × carton count / 1,000,000 to obtain cubic metres. Group A is 0.384 m³ and group B is 0.135 m³. Together they are seven cartons and 0.519 m³. Calculate each group separately; averaging the dimensions first does not preserve the volume.

State what the gross weight includes

In the same hypothetical example, each A carton weighs 12 kg gross and each B carton weighs 8 kg gross. The sum is 4 × 12 + 3 × 8 = 72 kg. This covers only the seven specified cartons. Adding a pallet, wrapping or an outer crate changes the final handling units and may change the dimensions and gross weight. Record the completed transport arrangement again. Keep product net weight, carton gross weight and pallet gross weight in separate fields.

Volume is not a confirmed freight charge

The cubic-metre result is a packing-volume record. It does not by itself determine chargeable weight or a final fee. Ask the quoting party to identify the unit, measurement method, minimum charge and conditions for the actual service. Air freight, sea freight, express services and individual quotations may use different bases. This guide supplies no universal volumetric-weight divisor, channel price or transport promise.

Send a versioned quotation pack

Attach the packing table to the goods description, collection and destination addresses, requested collection window, packing status, loading conditions and service scope to be confirmed. Disclose batteries, liquids or other characteristics that could affect acceptance and ask the service provider which documents are needed; a generic product name cannot establish acceptance. Packing photos can use the same carton references. Exclude unrelated identity documents and customer information from public material.

Compare the quote with the same input version

Ask the quoting party to name the packing-list version and date, carton count, weight, dimensions and service assumptions used. If sealing the order changes seven cartons to eight, or adds a pallet, send the complete revised table and confirm whether the earlier quote still applies. Retain the superseded file so the difference can be explained. A message saying only “updated data” leaves the recipient to guess which field changed.

Reconcile carton IDs before adding a new packing version

Give every handling unit a stable reference. If cartons A01–A04 are repacked into three larger cartons, mark the original four as superseded and create three replacement IDs. Do not leave both groups in the shipment total. Keep the list of goods moved between them so the order quantities can still be reconciled.

For an illustrative replacement, three cartons of 70 × 45 × 40 cm occupy 3 × 0.70 × 0.45 × 0.40 = 0.378 m³. Replacing the original four-carton group of 0.384 m³ changes the seven-carton example to six cartons and 0.378 + 0.135 = 0.513 m³. The gross weight must be measured again; a smaller carton count does not establish a lighter shipment.

Keep original units beside normalized dimensions

Do not mix centimetres and millimetres in the same multiplication. A carton recorded as 600 × 400 × 400 mm is 0.60 × 0.40 × 0.40 m, or 0.096 m³. Preserve the supplier's original dimensions and unit, then show the converted values in separate columns. Ask about a missing unit instead of guessing from the size of the number.

Retain sufficient precision for each row and add the calculated volumes before rounding the displayed total. The quotation may apply its own measurement or rounding rules; ask the provider to state them. Your geometric calculation is a cross-check of the packing inputs, not authority to replace the quotation basis.

Ask for a revised quote with the changed fields visible

Send the previous and replacement carton counts, dimensions, weights, packing date and goods references together. Ask which quotation lines change and which remain valid. If only some cartons are ready, identify that subset explicitly: the full-order total is not the volume of the first collection.

Basis and limits

The numbers are fully stated hypothetical inputs with reproducible arithmetic. The workflow is an editorial method for organising quotation data, not a carrier standard or a record of completed transport. The applicable quote and service documents must confirm the channel, charging basis and acceptance conditions for the actual goods.
